Mission Logs & Progress

Chronological operational timeline of DAWN's performance in Mars-analogue field operations.

Day 01 · Mission Expo

IRC Rover Expo & Arena Setup

Day 1 of the International Rover Challenge at Manipal Institute of Technology, Udupi, began with an expo-style showcase where teams presented their rover designs and engineering approaches. We actively engaged with fellow teams, enthusiastically explaining our rover’s minimal and efficient architecture while also exploring other systems through detailed discussions.

The international nature of the competition enabled meaningful interactions with students from Madras, Poland, and various other states, giving us exposure to diverse technical perspectives. The day concluded with a visit to the competition arena and a rover photo session at the mission site, reflecting our energy and excitement for the challenges ahead.

Beyond the technical exchange, the lively and collaborative atmosphere on campus fueled our enthusiasm. Constant interaction, curiosity, and teamwork throughout the day reinforced our motivation and set a strong momentum for the rest of the competition.

Day 02 · Autonomous Navigation

Autonomous Navigation & Terrain Traversal

Day 2 placed DAWN directly in the Mars-analogue test pit to validate autonomous waypoint navigation and obstacle evasion algorithms. The onboard Jetson computing cluster and stereo depth sensors continuously mapped the surrounding rock formations and guided the rover safely along the pre-programmed GPS coordinates.

The rocker-bogie suspension system excelled across loose gravel dunes and steep inclines, maintaining full tractive effort with zero wheel slip, affirming the rigorous pre-competition CAD simulation and finite-element modeling.

Day 03 · Manipulation & Science

Robotic Arm Manipulation & Science Mission

Day 3 focused on precise robotic arm manipulation and scientific soil sample extraction. DAWN's custom multi-DOF arm successfully reached the target maintenance interface to toggle switches, reconnect umbilical terminals, and collect surface core samples for onboard soil analysis testing.

Our science and electrical subsystems delivered crisp, uninterrupted telemetry back to the ground control station, completing the operational trial with outstanding marks from international judges.
